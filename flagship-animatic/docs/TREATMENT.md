# YOCHI flagship: creative treatment (working title **ONE OF MANY**)

Status: **creative development and animatic, for owner review.** Not a production render. Nothing published, merged
or deployed. THE LAST STAND, BATTLE ROYALE V4, the achievement work and the canonical art are untouched.

## The idea in one line

A brutal one-candle duel ends, the ink clears, and the camera keeps pulling back until we see the duel was one of
hundreds, then the whole world places its stake on a single candle.

## Audience and job

- **Who:** crypto-native players on X and YouTube who have never heard of Yochi, plus existing players. Muted-first,
  but built on the music.
- **Job:** stop the scroll with a fight, earn the scale reveal, then make "real players, real stakes, one pool" land
  without a tutorial. End on yochigg.xyz.
- **Tone:** action-manga, restrained Sumi brand. Drama from scale, silence and timing. Real money said plainly, never
  promised.

The two acts aren't stitched: one character carries the whole film, the same UP / DOWN grammar scales from two
people to two crowds, and the music is a single run from the groove to the track's own ending.

## Why the transition works (and what it needs)

The reveal is a multiplane dolly, not a zoom. Each plane has a depth and its own rate, so the world opens around her
rather than the picture shrinking. Stage 1 runs on existing art: the approved victory pose, matted, its paper turning
into the world's mist, with mist tracked to the painting's two crop lines. It holds to about half scale. Stage 2 needs
**two new images**: the same pose painted full-figure on a ledge (B01) and the layered world (B02). Without B01 the
reveal fails at the moment her legs should appear. That's the one hard limit we found. Details in `FEASIBILITY.md`.

## Option A vs Option B

### Option A · Duels → Battle Royale (rendered as the alternative cut)

Ten seats, 10 × 10 USDC, two lives each. Round 1 costs four players a life and nobody's out (the lives rule shown, not
told). Rounds 2–4 take seven players out, each knock-out an impact frame. Final two in the lighter section, Duel
Master against Bloodsport on their last lives. The slam closes it: last one standing takes 95.

### Option B · Duels → Free For All (**recommended**, rendered as the main cut)

As above: an open field, any stake, one pool, many winners.

### Comparison

| criterion | A · Battle Royale | B · Free For All |
|---|---|---|
| **Narrative coherence** | Strong progression (2 → 10 → 2 → 1), but the climax is **another 1v1**, so the film's biggest moment repeats its opening's shape. Duel Master winning twice reads as plot armour; Bloodsport winning makes the hero lose the flagship. | The duel's UP / DOWN becomes the whole world's UP / DOWN, the same grammar at scale. She carries through as one player among many, which is exactly the reveal's meaning ("one of many"). |
| **Visual spectacle** | Many small impacts (knock-outs). Less scale at the end (a table of tiles, then two people). | One huge impact: the strike line as a front line between two crowds, the close greying a whole side. The reveal leads straight into the biggest image in the film. |
| **Musical compatibility** | Good: rounds on drop 2's beats, final two in the lighter section, the slam on the close. | Equally good, and the entries on every second beat use drop 2's momentum better than rounds (which need a close, a result and a roster update each). |
| **Gameplay clarity** | Lives, rounds, elimination, the round cap and the replay rule all need showing. Doable (BR V4 proved it) but dense in 22 s. | Simpler: pick a side, put in what you want, picks close, one candle, the right side splits the pool. Multipliers and the pool make it visible without words. |
| **Financial stakes** | 100 pot, 95 to one person. Clear but small. | A 12,480 USDC pool; different stakes and different payouts on screen; losers lose their stakes in grey. It communicates pooled, proportional, real money, and that many can win. |
| **Memorable characters** | Good: each knock-out is a character moment (needs hit art per character). | Good: each entry is a character moment with a stake that suits them (High Roller 100, Lucky Fool 5). Rampage's return on the wrong side is a laugh and a lesson. |
| **New artwork** | B01, B02 + B06a/b, B07, B09 (+B08): **6–7 images**. | B01, B02 + B04 (high impact): **2–3 images** (+1 prep re-matte). |
| **Production complexity** | Higher: per-character impact frames, a roster UI with lives, four resolved rounds. | Lower: one field, one candle, entry slams built from the house cut-outs. |
| **Reuse of 2.5D components** | Cut-outs, hit-frame grammar from THE LAST STAND, BR V4's roster logic. | Cut-outs (every entrant is the house 2.5D layer), the versus band, the stamp, the rise. |
| **Flagship effectiveness** | A strong mode trailer. As a flagship it says "here's another fight". | Says "this is a world": the duel was the hook, the field is the product. |

**Recommendation: Option B, Free For All.** It earns the pullback (the reveal shows a world; FFA *is* the world),
it's the cheapest to finish (two essential images), and it shows what makes Yochi different (opposing predictions,
one pool, proportional payouts) more clearly than a second elimination fight. Battle Royale stays a viable treatment:
it's rendered as the alternative, on the same music and the same first two acts, and it's the better choice if you
want a mode trailer for BR specifically.

**A three-mode film?** Not recommended. Duels → FFA already shows both ends of Yochi (1v1 and everyone). Adding BR
would cost the lighter section's tension (or 15 more seconds) and turn the film into a feature tour. The end card names
all three modes instead.

## Decisions and open questions

1. **FFA candle direction.** Her duel pick was DOWN; in the FFA she picks UP and the candle closes UP. It avoids "she
   always picks DOWN" and makes Rampage's copied DOWN the joke. Reversible in `data.ts` / `FFA.tsx`.
2. **The real-money line.** The end card carries a placeholder ("Real USDC. You can lose your stake.") pending
   counsel's wording (OPEN_DECISIONS #11).
3. **FFA rules not re-verified against the app in this session** (see `FEASIBILITY.md` §5). The numbers are built to
   the app's own screens and are internally consistent; the contract checks are listed.
4. **Facing direction.** No painting is mirrored (it isn't an allowed operation, and it changes costume asymmetry).
   Some DOWN-side entrants therefore face away from the centre. Acceptable in the field; it matters only for a BR
   final two (B09).
5. **Ground switches:** paper (the duel's strike, the victory, the world) → sumi on bar 80 (the round starts) → paper
   on the final slam (identity, the end card). Each on a beat, each meaning something.
6. **Vermilion** only for the seal, the slash, 決/勝 seals, CALLED IT and the stroke (the USDC v3 exception, already
   pending sign-off in THE LAST STAND). Never on the losing side.

# YOCHI flagship v2: revised creative treatment

Working title **ONE OF MANY**. 79 s, 1920×1080, on the owner's dark DnB `Victory Stamp`.
**Status: storyboard and rough animatic for owner review.** No final-resolution production has started.

## The film in one paragraph

A brute and an assassin settle a one-minute candle on a lone stone spire. The candle turns on him, she strikes,
he falls, the ink clears to her standing alone. Then the camera won't stop retreating: back through a stone
gateway, out over a gorge, until she is a speck at its head and we see what she was standing above. Two terraced
cliffs face each other across the drop, and they're filling with players. A Free For All round is gathering.
We meet a laughing High Roller who puts 100 on UP because she likes being watched, a cold swordsman who puts 80 on
DOWN against the whole crowd, and a nobody with a lucky ticket who puts 5 on UP and immediately regrets it. Picks
lock. The candle opens on the drop and dives; DOWN is winning, and the High Roller's laugh freezes. Then it turns.
It claws back up through the build and rides the strike to the last second, and on the loudest slam in the track
it closes UP. The market's ink wave breaks over the DOWN cliff: the veteran goes to one knee, the card sharp loses
his hand, the cold swordsman lifts his mask over his face as the grey takes him. On the UP side the High Roller
cackles, the blindfolded swordswoman sheathes her blade without turning round, and the nobody stands in a rain of
coins with her mouth open: +6.58. On her spire, Duel Master has watched all of it. She turns and steps down. The next
candle is opening. The seal stamps.

## What changed from the 59.6 s animatic, and why

| v1 problem | v2 answer |
|---|---|
| The duel was a simplified rebuild, inferior to the approved short | **The opening is THE LAST STAND v2 itself**: its own scenes, art, camera, timing, sound and SFX stem, unchanged to the victory reveal. Only its closing lockup card is switched off, and the victory hold runs on into the reveal |
| The FFA act was a dashboard: entry cards, counters, multipliers | The pool is said **once** (one band, during the pick window). Everything else is faces, bodies and the candle. Stakes appear as small tags next to the people who placed them |
| Nobody to care about | **Three principals with arcs** (High Roller, Cold Blooded, Lucky Fool), plus four supporting players with one beat each, plus a crowd |
| Rampage came back to lose again | Rampage is **not** a loser in the second act. He appears once more, optionally, at the very end: back for the next round, fist forward, formidable |
| Duel Master won everything | **She doesn't play the round.** She is the witness: the film's eye, framing the world from her spire, and the last thing before the end card is her stepping down to play |
| The pullback was a shrinking picture over diagrams | A designed world (the gorge), a spire with ground under her feet (B01), a gateway the camera passes through, distant duels, two cliffs of players. Six painted plates, built as depth layers |
| The music's best material went to UI | Drop 3, the track's loudest drop, is the candle opening; the build is the reversal; the **outro slam is the close**, the final slam and last stab are the end card |
| One generic win/lose treatment | Every principal and supporting player wins or loses **in character** (see casting) |

**The emotional line.** Act I is fear and release (his), Act II is awe, the gathering is appetite, the pick window is
doubt, the candle is dread then hope, the close is consequence. The film ends on the idea that it happens again
every five minutes, and that you can be in it.

### The witness and the cameo

- **Duel Master** holds the film together without playing the round: the reveal is from her, the held breath and the
  last bar include her on her spire, and her last beat is turning from the view and stepping down (B26): she's next.
- **Rampage** (optional, B27): one shot on the far terrace as the next round opens, bandaged fist forward. He lost a
  duel, not his nerve. That keeps him formidable and sets up a rematch, without making him a running joke.

## FFA mechanics, kept honest

- Picks are made in a window **before** the candle opens, and lock; nobody enters mid-candle (L3/L4 show the lock).
- Any stake, either side; every player appears once, on one side.
- Players never attack each other. **The market is the only force**: the ink wave is the close's consequence landing
  on the losing side, a metaphor for the outcome, not a fight.
- Many winners at once; each wins in proportion to stake (5 → +6.58, 40 → +52.63, 100 → +131.56); losers lose their
  stakes, in grey. No lives, no survivor.
- **Every FFA number is a placeholder marked UNVERIFIED on screen** (the app repo couldn't be read this session). See
  `FEASIBILITY.md` §4. A currency variant is needed: USDC (yochigg.xyz) or HYPE (Elysium).

## Candid assessment

**What works now.**
- The structure. Duel → world → round → close gives the soundtrack's three biggest moments (drop 2's hit, drop 3,
  the outro slam) to the three biggest story beats (the strike, the candle opening, the close).
- The three principals give the round a point of view.
- The reversal rhymes with the duel's (up then down, now down then up) without repeating it.

**What doesn't yet.**
- The world is diagrams until E01–E06 exist; the reveal's power is entirely in those paintings and in B01.
- Several key beats are stand-ins (frozen laugh, eyes shut, mask on), so their emotional effect can only be judged
  from the briefs.
- The close has six reaction shots in 4 s, which may be one too many: drop one loser (Grudge Settled) if it feels
  rushed.

**What would make it exceptional rather than adequate.**
1. **The reveal must be one unbroken move.** From her face to the gateway framing the gorge, no cut. Only B01 at the
   hand-over is allowed, hidden under the mist. If it breaks, it's a slideshow.
2. **Lucky Fool's coins must be the emotional peak.** It's the only moment in the film where the money is small, and
   it should feel the biggest. Give it the stillest frame and the longest hold of the payoff.
3. **Cold Blooded's mask.** A loss that looks like a decision. It's the image people will remember from the second
   act; brief it with the most care.
4. **Restraint in the UI.** One band, said once. If it creeps back in, the film becomes the dashboard again.
5. **Silence.** The approved 3-beat silence works. A second, the last half-bar before the outro slam, would make
   the close land harder. That needs your edit (it's a music decision).
6. **Sound design.** Real ink, paper and crowd textures (OPEN_DECISIONS #12) would do more for the scale than any
   visual effect.
